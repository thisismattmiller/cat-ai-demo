## Applies when

- The schedule under the topic offers captions consisting of a date or span of dates, either
  for the period covered by the work (history of a topic by period) or for the publication
  date of the work (recent vs. older material).
- The work covers several of the listed spans, is a new edition with enlarged coverage, is a
  reprint or facsimile, or is a translation.

## Rules

1. Classification according to period covered. [sec. 1]
   a. Date spans in the schedules are established in a single list, closing each period before
      beginning the next; the closing date of one span is identical with the opening date of
      the next span (e.g. 1945-1958, 1958-1971, 1971-). A major period may be left open with
      minor periods listed underneath. If the earliest span has no opening date, its caption
      is "Through [date]" rather than "To [date]". [sec. 1.a]
   b. Assignment of numbers. [sec. 1.b]
      (1) General rule. Assign the class number that corresponds to the earliest period on
          which the work focuses when the work in hand covers several of the time spans listed
          under the topic in question. Ignore brief coverage of earlier periods that serves
          only to introduce the principal time period(s) being discussed.
          To [date]. Even though this formulation is not used when new date spans are
          proposed, there are many existing captions of this type in the schedules. Interpret
          them to mean up to but not including the date given in the caption.
      (2) Editions. Assign the same class number to the various editions of a work, provided
          the time period covered in each edition remains essentially the same. If in a new
          edition the time span covered is enlarged to focus first on a later time period
          provided for in the schedule, class with the later period even though this will
          result in the work being separated from earlier editions.
2. Classification according to date of publication. [sec. 2]
   a. Date spans in the schedules are established so that the closing date of one span does
      not overlap with the opening date of the next (e.g. Through 1800, 1801-1859, 1860-1900,
      1901-). If the earliest span has no opening date, the caption is "Through [date]"
      rather than "To [date]". [sec. 2.a]
   b. Assignment of numbers. [sec. 2.b]
      (1) General rule. Assign the class number that corresponds to the publication date of
          the work being cataloged.
          To [date]. Even though this formulation is not used when new date spans are
          proposed, there are many existing captions of this type in the schedules. Interpret
          them to mean up to but not including the date given in the caption.
      (2) Editions. Assign the class number that corresponds to the publication date of the
          edition being cataloged even if this results in the work being separated from its
          other editions. However, if a facsimile or reprint edition is being cataloged,
          assign the class number that corresponds to the publication date of the edition from
          which the reproduction was made.
      (3) Translations. Assign the class number that corresponds to the publication date of
          the translation being cataloged even if this results in the work being separated
          from the original work.

## Pitfalls

- Decide first which kind of date breakdown the schedule uses: period covered (sec. 1) or
  publication date (sec. 2); the rules for editions, reprints and translations differ.
- "To [date]" means up to but not including that date; "Through [date]" includes it
  [sec. 1.b.(1), 2.b.(1)].
- Under period-covered breakdowns, a work spanning several periods goes with the earliest
  period it focuses on, not the latest and not the longest [sec. 1.b.(1)].
- Under publication-date breakdowns, reprints and facsimiles take the ORIGINAL edition's
  date; new editions and translations take their OWN date [sec. 2.b.(2)-(3)].
- Overriding F 175 sec. 1 (same number for all editions) is expected here [sec. 1.b.(2),
  2.b.(2)].
