  biography, general works, etc.) at the head of a discipline, with the caption models for
  the D, H, Q and R schedules; used to read a schedule's preliminary section and to decide
  where a work of a given form belongs when no specific form number exists.

## Applies when

- A form number (periodicals, congresses, dictionaries, directories, biography, general works,
  handbooks, study and teaching, etc.) must be located within the preliminary section at the
  head of a topic.
- The schedule lacks a number for the specific form of the work in hand under its subject
  (F 10 sec. 2 refers here).
- Working in classes D, H, Q, or R, where the printed caption order follows one of the models
  below.

## Rules

1. General rule. For an orderly arrangement of topics in an individual discipline, provide
   first for a preliminary section specifying forms of publication and special aspects of the
   discipline as a whole, then for a logical breakdown of the discipline into subtopics.
   [sec. 1]
2. Preliminary captions vs. subtopics. Do not position subtopics of the discipline in the
   preliminary section, and do not intersperse preliminary captions among the subtopics,
   unless it is characteristic of a particular class to locate certain material contrary to
   these instructions. For example, in some classes provision for place is characteristically
   located at the end of a section for the discipline after all subtopics. [sec. 2]
3. Adding form captions. Add standard form captions under a topic upon receipt of the first
   appropriate item but only when one or more standard form captions already exist for the
   discipline in question or when preparing a major development. Examples of major
   developments are DK4000+, Poland; TJ807+, Solar energy; and TD1020+, Hazardous substance
   disposal. [sec. 3]
4. Classification models. Insofar as feasible, follow the models below, adopting the same
   order and captions within the specified schedules. The presence of topics on a model list
   does not mean that all topics must be established for a major development. The most
   important topics may be selected and other topics added later if needed. If no model for a
   class exists, follow precedent within the class for the order and captions. [sec. 4]

## Examples

Major developments in which a full set of form captions was established [sec. 3]:
`DK4000+` Poland; `TJ807+` Solar energy; `TD1020+` Hazardous substance disposal.

Museum subarrangement under each country in the H and R models (".x / .x2" Cutter pattern):
`.x  General works` / `.x2  Individual, by name, A-Z` (H); `.x2  Special. By city, A-Z` (R).

## Pitfalls

- The D model is not to be used for development in E and F [sec. 4 heading].
- Individual classes may characteristically deviate (e.g. place at the end after all
  subtopics); that established pattern wins over the model [sec. 2].
- The Q model's first caption is "Periodicals, societies, congresses, serial publications",
  so in class Q congresses are classed in the periodicals number (cf. F 240 sec. 1).
- In the H model, when individual biography classes with the country, only "Biography
  (Collective)" is provided.
