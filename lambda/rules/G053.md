## Applies when

- Every call number. This card defines the parts of a call number and where each part comes from.

## Rules

1. **Subject analysis** [sec. 1]. The cataloger analyzes the nature and contents of the item and reflects this analysis in two ways:
   - a. **Subject headings** [sec. 1.a]. The subject is described in words by one or more subject headings chosen from the Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH); names of individuals, corporate bodies or places may also be used as subject headings. Not every work receives a subject heading (individual literary works and sacred works, for example, usually do not). Subject headings are recorded in the 600-651 fields:
     - 600 = Personal name subject headings
     - 610 = Corporate name subject headings
     - 611 = Conference or meeting subject headings
     - 630 = Uniform title subject headings
     - 650 = Topical subject headings
     - 651 = Geographic subject headings
   - b. **Class number (classification number)** [sec. 1.b]. A class number is selected from the LC classification schedules, based on the subject matter and form of the item. The most common class numbers consist of one, two or three capital letters, up to four whole numbers, and decimal extensions. Some class numbers also contain Cutters reflecting subject matter, geographic area, or names, etc.

2. **Shelflisting** [sec. 2]. To the class number is added a *book number* that is based on instructions in the schedules, if any, and on the rules laid out in this manual. The purpose of the book number is to arrange works in one class number in a logical arrangement, usually alphabetically by the main entry. If other arrangements are preferred, they are specified in the schedules.
   - a. **Cutter number** [sec. 2.a]. In many cases the first element of a book number is a Cutter number, frequently based on the first word of the 1XX field, usually the author's surname. The Cutter number is the means by which an alphabetical arrangement of books is achieved. This alphabetical arrangement is based on the LC filing rules (see G 100) and the Preferred Shelflist Order table (see G 65).
   - b. **Date** [sec. 2.b]. Monographs shelflisted since April 1982 contain a date, normally the publication date, following the Cutter number that reflects the main entry.
   - c. **Other elements** [sec. 2.c]. Some call numbers may contain volume or part numbering, or other elements.

3. **Order of authority.** The call number of a book is based on (1) instructions in the classification schedules, (2) the rules in this manual, and (3) works previously cataloged in the same class number in the shelflist.

## Examples

Class numbers of typical shapes (letters; letters + whole number; decimal extension; subject/geographic Cutters; two Cutters) [sec. 1.b]:

```
D9        QK477.2        HF5549.5.T7        KFC1102.4        HN670.3.Z9C6
```

## Pitfalls

- The usual alphabetical-by-main-entry arrangement is only the default; where the schedule specifies a different arrangement (by date, by place, by subject, etc.), the schedule governs [sec. 2].
- A work with no subject heading (individual literary work, sacred work) still receives a class number and book number [sec. 1.a].
