## Applies when

- Every call number: before building the book number, the schedule line for the class number (and any caption above it, possibly many pages earlier) must be read for subarrangement instructions.
- The class number ends in a reserved Cutter range (.A1, .A2-4, .A5-Z), an A-Z breakdown, a Cutter listed in the schedule, an "Under each" table (.x, .x2, .x3 ...), a Table reference (Table P1, Table H77a, Table T5/T6), or a caption such as "By date", "Subarrange by author", "Subarrange by editor or compiler", "By city and museum, A-Z".

## Rules

1. **Confirm the class number against the schedule.** The schedule is consulted to ensure that the proper classification has been provided for the work in hand and to determine whether any special instructions for completing the call number are presented. Normally the first one or two subject headings on the bibliographic record reflect the same subject matter shown in the schedule. The words used in the subject heading and the words in the classification caption need not be identical (e.g. subject headings *Physical fitness for children* and/or *Physical education and training* versus the caption for GV481, *Calisthenics. Group exercises*).

2. **Names usually are not in the schedule.** The names of specific individuals, corporate bodies, or places that may appear in subject headings usually do not appear in the classification schedules. The schedule may simply provide a general caption, e.g., *Biography – Individual, A-Z*.

3. **Schedule instructions override the manual.** In addition to the classification number and the topic, the schedule may include instructions for the subarrangement of the class according to special tables or lists of Cutter numbers. These instructions always take precedence over the shelflisting procedures and tables normally used, such as the Regions and Countries Table (see G 300) or the Biography Table (see G 320).

4. **Look above the caption.** These instructions may appear on the caption line, several lines above the caption, or dozens of pages before the class number selected. Follow explicit instructions that may be found under a general caption at a higher level than the specific topic given.

5. **Decide the book-number shape from the caption** (see the ten illustrations under Examples):
   - No special instruction: a single Cutter based on the first word of the main entry (Illustration #1).
   - Reserved Cutter ranges (.A1, .A2-4, .A5-Z): periodicals and sources are double Cuttered within the reserved span; general works are Cuttered for the 1XX/245 but must fit within .A5-Z, so headings beginning with A get adjusted Cutters (Illustration #2).
   - A first Cutter printed in the schedule for form, subject, or place: add a second Cutter for the 1XX and/or 245 fields (Illustrations #3, #4, #5).
   - A second Cutter already derived from the subject (special railroads, special projects, place under a topical Cutter): the main entry can only be a digit or digits appended to the second Cutter, positioned to approximate the alphabetical place of the 1XX/245 (Illustrations #6, #7, #8).
   - "Under each" tables: `.x` is the Cutter selected for the item; `.x2` appends the digit 2 to it, `.x4A-Z` appends 4 and then a second Cutter for the region, etc.; the main entry is then a Cutter or a digit after that (Illustration #9).
   - Captions such as "By date", "Subarrange by date", "Subarrange by author", "Subarrange by editor or compiler", "By city and museum, A-Z", "Subarrange each by Table T6" change what the book number represents (Illustration #10).

## Examples

### Illustration #1: No special instruction: Single Cutter for main entry

```
CB       HISTORY OF CIVILIZATION                                CB

   4        Yearbooks
              Collected works (nonserial)
   5             Several authors
   7             Individual authors
```

No specific subarrangement instructions for these numbers are provided in the schedule. A single Cutter number based on the first word of the main entry is assigned to works classed in numbers like these.

### Illustration #2: Reserved Cutters

```
DS                ASIA                                          DS

                   Arab War, 1967
   127.A1            Periodicals.  Collections.
   127.A2-4          Sources.  Documents
   127.A5-Z          General works
```

This example presents three different Cuttering situations. The Cutters .A1-49 are reserved for special types of publications. General works use the range .A5-Z.

Periodicals are Cuttered for main entry, resulting in two Cutters appearing in the call number, which is called "double Cuttering."

In the span .A2-A4, the Cutter must fit in this limited span of .A2 to .A4999... The actual Cutter number selected depends on works already shelflisted in that number: the alphabetical position is determined and the number selected to make the entry file correctly and provide for space for future expansion.

A general work is Cuttered based on the 1XX and/or 245 fields, but when the field begins with A, the Cutter number must be adjusted to fit the limited range available. For example, the author Abbott could not receive the normal Cutter .A22. The Cutter number must begin with .A5, so .A52 could be chosen. If the first work in this class is by Andrews, the Cutter number .A53 should not be chosen; .A63 would be preferable in order to leave space for authors with names beginning Ab-Am.

### Illustration #3: Double Cutter--second Cutter for 1XX and/or 245 fields

```
HV   SOCIAL PATHOLOGY.  SOCIAL AND PUBLIC WELFARE.  CRIMINOLOGY   HV

                   Victims of crimes.  Victimology
   6250.4.A-Z        Special classes of persons as victims, A-Z
   6250.4.A34          Aged.  Older people
```

This example is similar to DS127.A1 above. In both cases, a first Cutter number represents the form of material or the subject of the book. It is printed in the schedule. A second Cutter number is assigned for the 1XX and/or 245 fields.

### Illustration #4: Double Cutter--second Cutter for 1XX and/or 245 fields

```
HD        INDUSTRIES.  LAND USE.  LABOR                          HD

                   Labor.  Work.  Working class
                     Hours of labor
                       Weekly rest day.  Sunday work
   5114.A3               General works
   5114.A5-Z             By region or country, A-Z
```

This example is similar to the previous one: For a general work, the first Cutter (.A3) has already been specified in the schedule as a breakdown of the class number. A second Cutter is assigned for the 1XX and/or 245 fields.

The .A5-Z will also result in two Cutter numbers, the first for the geographic area and the second for the 1XX and/or 245 fields. For countries beginning with the letter A, the Cutters need to be adjusted to accommodate the smaller span allowed.

### Illustration #5: Double Cutter--second Cutter for 1XX and/or 245 fields

```
F                        UNITED STATES LOCAL HISTORY              F

                   Virginia
                     By period
  231                  1865-1930.  Reconstruction
                          Biography: William Mahone, John Edward
                             Massey
```

This number is not considered to be a "biography number" (See F 275 and G 320.) Biographies classed in this number are, however, double Cuttered: the first Cutter represents the biographee, as indicated by the first subject heading. Because this is not a "biography number", no table is used. The second Cutter represents the 1XX and/or 245 fields. This results in the presence of some single-Cuttered works in this class number, representing works simply about that historical period, and some double-Cuttered works, representing biographies.

### Illustration #6: Double Cutter--digit added for 1XX and/or 245 fields

```
TF                    ELECTRIC RAILWAYS                          TF

                   Electrification of steam railroads
   859.A-Z4          Special cities (Terminals, etc.), A-Z
   859.Z5A-Z         Special railroads, A-Z
```

Because the second Cutter is derived from the name of the railroad to represent the subject, it is necessary to extend the digits of the second Cutter to represent the main entry. The numbers selected depend on the items already shelflisted. If a railroad is used for the first time, the last digit should approximate the position of the contents of the 1XX and/or 245 fields in the alphabet. For example, if TF859.Z5P46 represents electrification of the Pennsylvania Railroad, then the author Carleton could be represented by a 3, resulting in TF859.Z5P463.

### Illustration #7: Double Cutter--digit added for 1XX and/or 245 fields

```
P                    PHILOLOGY.  LINGUISTICS                     P

                   Relation to sociology.  Sociolinguistics
  40                 General works
  40.5.A-Z          Special aspects, A-Z
  40.5.L35             Language planning  Table P1
```

It is necessary to consult the table, which reads:

```
.x      General works
.x2A-Z  By region or country, A-Z
```

General works use .L35 and a second Cutter is added for the author or title. However, if the work is limited to a specific place, the digit 2 is added to the topical Cutter and the second Cutter is used for the place. The 1XX and/or 245 fields can therefore be represented only by a digit. For example, language planning in Ireland classes in P40.5.L352I75, with L352 reflecting the alphabetical listing of countries and I75 representing Ireland. A work by O'Laorie would use the digit 6, resulting in P40.5.L353I756.

### Illustration #8: Double Cutter--digit added for 1XX and/or 245 fields

```
TL      MOTOR VEHICLES. AERONAUTICS. ASTRONAUTICS                TL

               Artificial satellites
                 By country, region, etc. A-Z
                   United States
 796.5.U5             General works
 796.5.U6A-Z          Special projects or programs, A-Z
      e.g.
 796.5.U6V3   Vanguard
```

An individual work about the Vanguard program would use digits to show the alphabetical order of the 1XX and/or 245 fields. For example, a work by Green is Cuttered .U6V35, and a work by Stehling is Cuttered .U6V37.

### Illustration #9: "Under each" subarrangements: .x and .x2

```
HV    SOCIAL PATHOLOGY.  SOCIAL AND PUBLIC WELFARE.  CRIMINOLOGY    HV

               Protection, assistance and relief
                 Special classes
                   Older people
                     By region or country
                       Other regions or countries
 1478.A-Z                Other American regions or countries, A-Z
                             Under each country:
     .x     Periodicals.  Societies.  Serials
     .x2    General works.  History
     .x3    Other
     .x4A-Z By region or state, A-Z
     .x5A-Z By city, A-Z
```

The notation ".x" refers to the Cutter number selected for the item being shelflisted. .x2 means that the digit 2 is appended to that Cutter number. For example, the Cutter number for Venezuela in G 300, used in the absence of conflict in the shelflist, is .V4. A general work on older people in Venezuela would be HV1478.V42. A Cutter number for the 1XX and/or 245 fields would then be added. For a work on the elderly in Caracas, the call number would begin with HV1478.V45C37, and the author would be reflected in the digit after the 7.

## Pitfalls

- Schedule instructions always take precedence over the manual's default tables (G 300 Regions and Countries Table, G 320 Biography Table) [Background].
- The governing instruction may be printed far above the class number, under a higher-level caption; do not stop reading at the caption line [Background].
- In a reserved range such as .A5-Z, a heading beginning with A cannot take its table Cutter (.A22 for Abbott); it must be forced into the span (.A52), leaving room below for names that file earlier (Illustration #2).
- A first Cutter for the biographee in a number like F231 does not make it a "biography number"; no Biography Table is applied, and the number holds a mix of single- and double-Cuttered works (Illustration #5; see F 275 and G 320).
- Where the second Cutter already carries subject meaning (railroad, project, place), never add a third Cutter for the main entry; append a digit chosen to approximate its alphabetical position (Illustrations #6-#9).
- In an "under each" table, `.x2A-Z`/`.x4A-Z` means: digit appended to the item's Cutter, then a second Cutter for the region/city, and the main entry is only a further digit (Illustrations #7, #9).
