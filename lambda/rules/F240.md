## Applies when

- The work is the collected papers, condensations, proceedings, program statements, or
  delegate lists of one or more named or unnamed congresses, symposia, conferences, meetings,
  etc., or a combination of these; whether entered under the congress name, a corporate body,
  or title.
- The work is about a congress.
- The record is a serial that continues an earlier serial congress record.

## Rules

1. Scope. Numbers for congresses are used to class (1) the collected papers delivered at or
   published for one or more named or unnamed congresses, symposia, conferences, meetings,
   etc.; (2) condensations of these papers; (3) reports of the proceedings and discussions,
   program statements, lists of delegates, etc., of congresses, or (4) combinations of these
   various types of materials. Publications that are entered under the name of an organization
   and that report on the proceedings of a business meeting of the organization with little or
   no substantive material on a topic are not classed as congresses but are treated as society
   publications and classed with the organization in the appropriate number for societies (cf.
   F 225). [Background]
2. Number established for congresses. If a number for congresses has been established, class
   both monographic and serial congresses in that number. Do not class congresses in the
   number for periodicals unless congresses are explicitly included in the caption for that
   number, as in class Q. [sec. 1]
3. No number established for congresses. If no number has been established for congresses,
   class congresses in the General works number (or in the periodicals number if the congress
   is cataloged as a serial). [sec. 2]
4. New numbers for congresses. Establish a new number for congresses only under the conditions
   described in F 195, sec. 4. Locate the number in accordance with the pattern for the
   schedule involved. Use as the caption only the word Congresses unless the schedule normally
   uses other wording. [sec. 3]
5. Monographic congresses. Class each monographic congress according to its subject matter.
   If the subject matter of a particular named congress varies from one meeting to another and
   separate bibliographic records are created for each meeting, do not attempt to keep the
   various meetings together in the same number. [sec. 4]
6. Continuations of serial congresses. When two or more serial records represent
   continuations of the same congress, follow the general rules for serial continuations,
   i.e., if the numbering continues, class all the continuations in the same number, and if
   the numbering does not continue, treat them as independent works. [sec. 5]
7. Works about a congress. Class such works in the same number that has been or would be
   assigned to the congress itself. Assign as the first subject heading the name of the
   congress. [sec. 6]
8. Shelflisting. For shelflisting procedures (Cutter and date for congresses), see G 230.
   [sec. 7]

## Pitfalls

- Serial congresses go in the Congresses number, not the periodicals number, when a
  Congresses number exists [sec. 1]; only when there is none does a serial congress fall to
  the periodicals number [sec. 2].
- Class Q's caption "Periodicals, societies, congresses, serial publications" includes
  congresses, so in Q they class in that number [sec. 1].
- Business-meeting proceedings with little substantive content are society publications
  (F 225), not congresses [Background].
- Successive meetings of a named congress with differing subjects and separate records are
  classed separately by subject [sec. 4].
