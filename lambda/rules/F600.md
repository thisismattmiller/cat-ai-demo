## Applies when
- The work is about genealogy, genealogical methodology or research, or is a collection of genealogical records.
- The work is the genealogy of an individual family, or a family history covering more than one country.
- The work is family biography (life stories of individual family members) or a mixture of family biography and genealogy.
- The work is church records, census materials, ship passenger lists, etc., that may be presented for genealogical use.

## Rules
1. General. Class general works about genealogy, including works on genealogical methodology, research, etc., in CS1-CS24. [sec. 1]
2. By place.
   - a. United States. Class works about American genealogy and general collections of United States genealogical records in CS42-CS68. Class collections of local genealogical material with the place in F. Class genealogical materials pertaining to an individual ethnic group with the group in E or F. [sec. 2.a]
   - b. Other countries. Class works about the genealogy of other countries and collections of records from these countries, including local genealogy and the genealogy of ethnic groups, in CS80-CS2209. [sec. 2.b]
3. Genealogy vs. other subjects. Many types of materials such as church records, census materials, passenger lists from ships, etc., are of interest to genealogists. In deciding where to class a work, let the intent of the editor or the publisher and the editing and indexing of the material serve as a guide. Generally, if the material is being presented for use by genealogists, class the work with genealogy. For local court records, see F 635. [sec. 3]
4. Individual families. Class genealogy of an individual family in CS, in the family history number under the country in which the family resides, e.g., CS71, United States. [sec. 4]
   - Class works of family history covering more than one country in CS38-CS39. If one of the countries included is the United States, class the work with family history under the United States. [sec. 4]
   - Class works of family biography focusing on the details of the life stories of individual family members in the appropriate biography number [sec. 4]:
     - in CT if the family members are not well-known persons or if they are prominent in several fields;
     - in D, E, or F if the family is important in the history of the country in which it resides;
     - with the appropriate topic if the family members are prominent in a special field, e.g., Wyeth family in art; Bach family in music.
   - If a work is a mixture of family biography and genealogy, class the work with genealogy, unless the genealogical material is clearly a minor part of the work. [sec. 4]

## Examples
Local United States genealogical material classes with the place in F [sec. 2.a]:
```
F180   Maryland
```

Genealogy of an individual American ethnic group classes with the group [sec. 2.a]:
```
E184.G3   German Americans
```

Individual family in the family history number for its country [sec. 4]:
```
CS71   United States
```

Family biography of a family prominent in a special field [sec. 4]:
Wyeth family in art; Bach family in music.

## Pitfalls
- The U.S. exceptions (local genealogy in F, ethnic-group genealogy in E or F) do not apply to other countries, whose local and ethnic genealogy stays in CS80-CS2209. [sec. 2]
- A multi-country family history that includes the United States goes under the United States, not CS38-CS39. [sec. 4]
- Mixed family biography and genealogy defaults to genealogy unless the genealogy is clearly minor. [sec. 4]
