## Applies when

- The work relates an individual or group of individuals to a topic: a biography,
  autobiography, memoir, diary, letters, speeches/interviews, collected or selected writings
  of a person, or criticism of a person's work or views.
- The class number is (or could be) a "Biography" number, "Biography (Collective)",
  "Individual, A-Z", or "Biography and memoirs" caption, or a number subarranged by an
  individual biography table.
- The work is a travel account, a joint biography (husband and wife, etc.), a family history,
  or a biography of a person with several careers.

## Rules

1. General rule. Class biographical works, individual or collective, with the topic with
   which the biographee is most closely identified. Use the special biography numbers provided
   under the topic. These numbers are used for any work whose purpose it is to relate an
   individual (or group of individuals) to a topic; such works may give personal details, but
   may also merely discuss the individual's activities, contributions, views, etc., relating to
   the topic. Class in CT (General biography) only works on individuals not identified with a
   special topic, and works of the type described in sec. 5.b, below. [Background; sec. 1]
2. Establishing biography numbers. Differing traditions have developed for the various
   classification schedules. Establish biography numbers according to the normal practices
   followed for the schedule in question. Examples: [sec. 2]
   - In some schedules, such as the history schedules, it is customary to establish biography
     numbers under specific subtopics the first time they are required.
   - In some schedules, such as Class Q, it is customary to use the biography numbers provided
     under major disciplines or topics rather than creating biography numbers under highly
     specific subtopics.
   - In some schedules it is customary to class biography in the General works number when an
     expansion of the form caption area is not justified (cf. F 195).
3. Form of captions. When establishing biography numbers, provide numbers for both individual
   and collective biography, with the standard captions "Biography / Collective / Individual,
   A-Z" unless the schedule consistently uses other standard captions. Avoid using Cutter
   numbers to split a single class number; use decimals instead (see Examples). [sec. 3]
4. Collective vs. individual biography. [sec. 4]
   a. Collective biography. Class as collective biography a work that discusses the lives
      and/or contributions of two or more individuals, if each individual is given substantial
      treatment in the work. Biographies that are about a single individual but give passing
      mention to friends, associates, etc., are not collective biographies. (Note: Until 2013,
      a discrete section of the work had to be devoted to each individual for the work to be
      considered a collective biography.) [sec. 4.a]
   b. Individual biography. Class as individual biography a continuous narrative that
      discusses the life of a single individual or the contributions of a single individual to
      a particular topic. Also class an autobiography as individual biography. Treat as
      individual biography works of the type My life with [a famous person] as well as combined
      biographies of two closely related people, such as a husband and wife, brother and
      sister, etc. Class such works with the more illustrious person, unless the purpose of the
      work is clearly to describe the life of the latter, in which case class with the latter.
      Accounts of travels: do not, for classification purposes, treat accounts of travels as
      biographical or autobiographical unless a substantial portion of the text, i.e. 50% or
      more, is devoted to personal details of the life of the traveler. [sec. 4.b]
   c. Single continuous text in which several disparate lives are discussed. Class according
      to the intent of the author or publisher. If the apparent intent is to tell a single
      story with stress on one person, class the work as a biography of that person. If the
      intent is to discuss several lives in relation to a topic, family, place, etc., class the
      work as collective biography. [sec. 4.c]
   d. Biographies of the members of one family. Class as collective biography unless the
      schedule in question makes special provision for individual families. (Note: Until 2013,
      biographies of the members of a single family were classed as individual biographies
      unless other provisions were made in the schedules.) [sec. 4.d]
5. Individual biography of persons with multi-faceted careers. [sec. 5]
   a. Emphasis on one career. If the biography emphasizes one particular career, class the
      work with that topic. For example, class a biography of Albert Schweitzer as a musician
      in ML; Leonardo da Vinci as a scientist in Q. [sec. 5.a]
   b. Discussion of several careers. If the biography discusses several careers in which an
      individual was engaged, class with the topic with which the biographee is most closely
      associated. If, according to standard reference sources, no single career is predominant
      in the life of the individual, as in the case of Albert Schweitzer, class the work in CT.
      [sec. 5.b]
6. Individual biography in history schedules. [sec. 6]
   a. Where to class individual biography. Class individual biography with special periods,
      reigns, or places following these principles: [sec. 6.a]
      (1) General history numbers. Do not include provisions for individual biography with
          collective biography under the general history numbers of an individual country.
      (2) Local history numbers. Class individual biography with local history unless the
          person was more prominent on the national than on the local level.
      (3) Reigns, administrations, etc. Class biographies of persons of national stature with
          the Biography and memoirs number under a particular monarch's reign, a particular
          president's administration, etc. Alternatively, it is possible to class biographies
          of this type with numbers established under larger periods of a nation's history
          when it is not practical to group on the basis of individual rulers. For further
          information on biographies of heads of state and legislators, see F 605, sec. 1.
      (4) Time periods under local history. Where individual localities are further
          subdivided by periods, including those using special tables inserted in the scheme,
          class individual biography with the appropriate period under local history.
   b. Listing the names of individuals. In general, individual persons are not listed under
      biography numbers in the schedules; exceptions are made to illustrate scope by a
      representative sampling, to indicate persons with multi-faceted careers (entered in more
      than one location in the classification), and to bring out prominent public figures,
      long-reigning monarchs, outstanding prime ministers, etc. [sec. 6.b]
7. Individual biography tables. Works classed in biography numbers may be further subarranged
   in accordance with an individual biography table. Some schedules, such as Classes E, N, and
   P, provide unique tables applicable to the specific schedule. For those classes for which a
   unique table is not provided, the general table below is used, provided that the name of
   the biographee is on the first Cutter. The principles in the table's "Including" notes are
   to be followed in biography tables in all classes. [sec. 7]
   Note: When this general table is applied, the numbers for Collected works, Selected works,
   and Speeches, essays, and lectures are used only when the collection by the individual
   biographee is so general that the work cannot be classed with a specific topic and would
   otherwise be classed in A, such as the collected writings of a journalist on several topics.
   The table is more commonly applied for autobiography, correspondence, or biography and
   criticism.
8. Shelflisting. For shelflisting procedures (formulating the Cutters and date), see G 320.
   [sec. 8]

## Tables

Biography table (biographee on first Cutter) [sec. 7]:

| Cutter | Content |
|---|---|
| .x | Cutter for the individual |
| .xA2 | Collected works. By date |
| .xA25 | Selected works. Selections. By date. Including quotations |
| .xA3 | Autobiography, diaries, etc. By date |
| .xA4 | Letters. By date |
| .xA5 | Speeches, essays, and lectures. By date. Including interviews |
| .xA6-Z | Biography and criticism. Including criticism of selected works, quotations, autobiography, letters, speeches, etc. |

## Examples

Standard captions for biography numbers, splitting by decimal rather than by Cutter [sec. 3]:
```
            Biography
915.49        Collective
915.5.A-Z     Individual, A-Z
```
not:
```
            Biography
915.A1        Collective
915.A3-Z      Individual, A-Z
```

Multi-faceted career, emphasis on one career [sec. 5.a]:
A biography of Albert Schweitzer as a musician classes in `ML`; of Leonardo da Vinci as a
scientist in `Q`.

No predominant career [sec. 5.b]:
A biography of Albert Schweitzer covering his several careers classes in `CT`.

Collection so general it would otherwise class in A [sec. 7 note]:
The collected writings of a journalist on several topics take the `.xA2` (Collected works)
number of the biography table.

## Pitfalls

- CT is only for persons not identified with any topic, or with no predominant career per
  standard reference sources [sec. 1, 5.b].
- Travel accounts are biography only when 50% or more of the text is personal detail of the
  traveler's life [sec. 4.b].
- Joint biographies of closely related people are individual biography, classed with the more
  illustrious person unless the work is clearly about the other [sec. 4.b].
- Family biographies are collective (since 2013) unless the schedule provides for individual
  families [sec. 4.d].
- The general table applies only when the biographee is on the first Cutter; E, N and P have
  their own tables [sec. 7].
- .xA2 / .xA25 / .xA5 are for topic-less collections only; the table is mainly for
  autobiography (.xA3), letters (.xA4) and biography/criticism (.xA6-Z) [sec. 7 note].
- Individual biography does not go with collective biography under a country's general
  history numbers; use the period, reign, or local number [sec. 6.a].
