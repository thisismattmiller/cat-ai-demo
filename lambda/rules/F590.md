## Applies when
- The work is folklore: items of culture learned orally, by imitation, or by observation, including beliefs, narratives (tales, legends, proverbs, etc.), folk medicine, and other aspects of the expressive performance and communication of the oral tradition.
- The work is a collection of, or criticism of, folk tales, legends, fairy tales, children's folklore (nursery rhymes, jump rope rhymes), folk drama, folk poetry, ballads, folk song lyrics, fables, proverbs, riddles, jokes, or toasts.
- The work concerns folk dance, folk music, folk customs and material culture, or folk art.

## Rules
1. Literature.
   - a. General rule. Class works on folklore and folk literature, including collections and criticism of folk literature, in GR. [sec. 1.a]
   - b. Folk tales, legends, fairy tales, etc. Class collections and criticism of folk tales, legends (except legends of medieval European origin), fairy tales, and children's folklore (nursery rhymes, jump rope rhymes, etc.) for adult audiences, in GR. In addition to medieval European legends, certain folk literature collections that have become part of a specific national literary canon and certain classics of folk literature are regarded as literature and classed as such in the P schedule. [sec. 1.b]
   - c. Genres of folk literature. Class works limited to texts and/or criticism of folk drama, folk poetry, ballads, lyrics of folk songs, fables, proverbs, riddles, jokes, or toasts in the appropriate areas of the P schedules. [sec. 1.c]
   - d. Works by literary authors. Class works that resemble folk literature but are created or adapted by individual literary authors with the author's works in P. Take care to determine that the work is the author's creation and not merely folk literature that a compiler collected and recorded. [sec. 1.d]
   - e. Works intended for children. Class single folk tales and legends or collections of folk tales and legends intended for children in PZ. [sec. 1.e]
   - f. Native peoples of North, South, and Central America. Class collections and criticism of the folklore and folk literature of the native peoples of North, South, and Central America in E-F. Class the folk literature of other native peoples as any other literature. [sec. 1.f]
2. Folk dance. Class works on folk dance in GV. [sec. 2]
3. Folk music. Class publications of folk music in M1627-M1853. Class works about folk music in ML3544-ML3776. Class texts of folk songs without the music in P. [sec. 3]
4. Folk customs and material culture. Class works on customs and material culture of individual groups with the specific ethnic group (see F 587) or geographic area in the D, E, or F schedules (usually in the class number for social life and customs). Class works on customs and material culture of folk societies and native peoples collectively or by geographic area in GN (Ethnology). Class works on certain specific customs and items of material culture, e.g., marriage customs, costumes, festivals, etc., in GT (Manners and customs). [sec. 4]
5. Folk art. Class works on folk art in N. [sec. 5]

## Examples
Folk literature classics regarded as literature and classed in P [sec. 1.b, Note]:
```
PL787.K6            Konjaku monogatari in Japanese literature
PT7262.5M63-633     Möðruvallabók in Old Norse literature
PK3741.S86          Śukasaptati in Sanskrit literature
```

Folk-like work created or adapted by an individual literary author [sec. 1.d]:
```
PQ1877   Charles Perrault's fairy tales
```

## Pitfalls
- Legends of medieval European origin are excluded from GR and go to P. [sec. 1.b]
- The genre exception (folk drama, poetry, ballads, song lyrics, fables, proverbs, riddles, jokes, toasts) sends works to P even though they are folk literature. [sec. 1.c]
- Children's folklore (nursery rhymes etc.) for adult audiences is GR; folk tales intended for children are PZ. [secs. 1.b, 1.e]
- Verify authorship before using sec. 1.d; a compiler who merely collected and recorded folk literature is not a literary author. [sec. 1.d]
- Folklore of native peoples of the Americas is E-F, not GR. [sec. 1.f]
- Folk song texts without music are P, not M. [sec. 3]
