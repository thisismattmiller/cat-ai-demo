  Whether a publication containing works by different literary authors is classed in the
  Collections number for its language/form/subject/date or as an edition of one
  predominant work (which requires an authorized access point for that work in a 7XX).
---

## Applies when

- The publication consists of two or more independent works by different literary authors not written specifically for the publication in hand, regardless of whether it has a collective title (DEFINITION).
- A single literary work is published together with one or more non-literary works.

## Rules

1. General rule. Class literary collections in the Collections number that reflects the language, literary form, subject matter, and/or date of the publication, unless the following two criteria both apply: [sec. 1]
   - One of the works is clearly predominant and the publication is clearly intended to be an edition of that work
   - The bibliographic record includes an authorized access point for the predominant work in a 7XX field

   If the above two criteria both apply, class as an edition of the predominant work.

2. Assignment of subject headings. When classing the work as a collection, assign headings appropriate to the collection. When classing the collection as an individual work, assign headings only as appropriate to that work. [sec. 2]

3. Single literary work published with one or more non-literary works. If one literary work is published in a collection with one or more non-literary works, class it as an individual literary work provided that the literary work is clearly the most important aspect of the publication. [sec. 3]

## Examples

Predominant work with a 700 name/title access point: classed as an edition of that work [sec. 1]:

```
050 00 $a PA4413 $b .A7 [date]
245 00 $a Antigone / $c Sophocles. Introduction to the Greek theatre / by
          P.E. Easterling.
700 02 $a Sophocles. $t Antigone.
700 12 $a Easterling, P. E. $t Introduction to the Greek theatre.
```

## Pitfalls

- Both criteria are required: a predominant work alone is not enough; the record must also carry a 7XX authorized access point for it [sec. 1].
- Lack of a collective title no longer routes a collection to the first-named work's author number (pre-RDA practice described in BACKGROUND).
- A literary work bound with non-literary works is classed as an individual literary work only when it is clearly the most important aspect [sec. 3].
