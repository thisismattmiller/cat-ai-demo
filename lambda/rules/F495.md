## Applies when
- The resource is an archival inventory: an inventory of the records and papers of government bodies, families, or academic and religious institutions (legal records, business records, government reports and papers, correspondence to or from an individual or institution, maps, news clippings, photographs, etc.).
- The resource is a catalog of manuscripts: a list of the handwritten or typewritten writings of an author or authors.
- The resource contains the text of documents from an archive or manuscript collection.

## Rules
1. Comprehensive archival inventories. Assign the appropriate number in CD to [sec. 1]:
   - Comprehensive inventories of the materials deposited in national, state, provincial, or municipal archives.
   - Comprehensive inventories of the archives of government bodies, including government departments or ministries and the bureaus, divisions, or agencies within them.
   - Inventories of the archives of several families or an individual family.
   - Comprehensive inventories of the archives of academic or religious institutions.
2. Archival inventories limited to a particular topic. Assign the appropriate number in class K, M or Z to special catalogs of archival materials limited to a particular topic. Distinguish between a comprehensive archival inventory that may incidentally have a broad topic and a selective inventory intentionally compiled to focus only on a special topic. [sec. 2]
3. Manuscript catalogs. Assign the appropriate number in Z6605-6621 and an alternate bracketed number in the regular classes to [sec. 3]:
   - Catalogs of manuscripts in one language.
   - Catalogs of manuscripts held by an individual library.
   - Catalogs of the manuscripts or archives of an individual.
   - Catalogs of manuscripts limited to a particular subject.
   Note: When Cuttering works classified in Z6611 by subject, Cutter for the broad topic involved, and not for its subtopics. [sec. 3, Note]
4. Documents from an archival or manuscript collection. Assign the appropriate number for sources in the regular classes to works containing the text of documents from an archive or manuscript collection. [sec. 4]

## Examples
Comprehensive archival inventory that incidentally has a broad topic [sec. 2]:
Title: Catalog of the archives of the Bureau of Indian Affairs. (CD3035.B8)

Selective inventory intentionally limited to a special topic [sec. 2]:
Title: Catalog of materials on the Seminole Indians located in the archives of the Bureau of Indian Affairs. (Z1210.S5)

Subject Cutter in Z6611 uses the broad topic [sec. 3, Note]:
```
.P5  Philosophy  [not .L8  Logic]
```

## Pitfalls
- A comprehensive inventory of an agency's archives stays in CD even if the agency's remit gives it a broad topic; only a selective, deliberately topic-focused inventory goes to K, M or Z. [sec. 2]
- Manuscript catalogs get two numbers: Z6605-6621 plus a bracketed alternate number in the regular classes. [sec. 3]
- In Z6611 the subject Cutter is for the broad topic (e.g., .P5 Philosophy), never a subtopic (.L8 Logic). [sec. 3, Note]
- Texts of the documents themselves are sources, classed in the regular classes, not in CD or Z. [sec. 4]
