## Applies when

- The resource is selected for the Rare Book and Special Collections Division, i.e. it falls into one of the categories below: books and non-book materials having unusual monetary value, aesthetic significance (unusually good quality type or design), and/or potential rarity (certain limited editions); books and non-book materials of historical significance because of provenance, an early imprint date, or because they represent an important contribution to American or Western civilization (e.g., the first book on accounting in America, the first book on the telegraph, etc.).
- The imprint date/place matches one of the categories in the tables below (pre-1801; early American imprints by state; Confederate imprints 1861-1865; Latin American imprints before 1820).

## Rules

1. Materials selected for the Rare Book and Special Collections Division are shelflisted following the provisions of the Classification and Shelflisting Manual (as well as internal division procedures). No separate call-number construction applies: build the call number by the general rules for the class and the form of the resource.
2. **Category 1. Pre-1801 imprints.** Books printed anywhere before 1801 are rare materials. Pre-1801 imprints are cataloged and shelflisted by staff in the Special Materials Cataloging Division [Procedures sec. 2].
3. **Category 2. American imprints.** American imprints published before the dates indicated in the table below are rare materials.
4. **Category 3. Imprints of the Confederate states during the Civil War, 1861-1865** (states listed below) are rare materials.
5. **Category 4. Latin American imprints.** Imprints published before 1820 are rare materials.

## Pitfalls

- Rarity changes custody and processing, not the structure of the call number; apply the class's normal Cuttering, dating and work-letter rules.
- Illinois imprints are rare before 1850 except Chicago (1871); New York State and Pennsylvania cutoffs (1830) exclude New York City and Philadelphia respectively.
