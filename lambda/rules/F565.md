## Applies when
- The resource is published in book or magazine format and has all of the following: the characters and action are portrayed in a succession of panel drawings; a series of panels advances a fictional narrative line; the dialogue is generally presented in "balloons" or their equivalent (in some cases the narrative is advanced with no dialogue or written material at all, or with written material at the bottom of the panels, e.g., Prince Valiant).
- It may be a single continuous narrative created for the publication in hand or compiled after previous publication elsewhere (including comic strip adaptations from other media such as motion pictures, dramas, or novels, even those using the exact words of the original), or a collection of several separate narratives (many short, e.g., four-panel, narratives or a few extended ones).
- The resource has some but not all of these characteristics (single panel cartoons, illustrated prose fiction, juvenile picture books in comic format, nonfiction in comic style, photo novels).

## Rules
1. General rule. Class comic books and graphic novels, as defined above, in the appropriate numbers in PN6700-PN6790 (for works whose primary intended audience is over age 15 or 9th grade), or in PZ (for works intended for juveniles through age 15, or 9th grade, e.g., PZ7.7 for American and English). Do not class comic books or graphic novels in numbers for individual literary authors in the PB-PT schedules. [sec. 1]
2. Other materials that do not fully conform to the definition in sec. 1 [sec. 2]:
   - a. Collections of single panel cartoons, single photographs with balloon captions, etc., that have no continuing narrative line: class in NC1300-NC1763. [sec. 2.a]
   - b. Fictional narratives that consist essentially of text written in book style with occasional illustrations employing comic characters (with or without balloon dialogue): class as literary works, i.e., in individual literary author numbers, or in PZ if juvenile. [sec. 2.b]
   - c. Works primarily intended to be juvenile fiction or picture books but presented in cartoon or comic strip format: class in PZ as juvenile belles lettres. [sec. 2.c]
   - d. Nonfiction works written in comic book style: class in the appropriate topical numbers in classes A-Z. [sec. 2.d]
   - e. "Photo novels," i.e., novels that consist almost entirely of photographs (with or without balloon dialogue): class in the numbers for comic books, strips, etc., in PN6700-PN6790. [sec. 2.e]

## Pitfalls
- The audience split is the publisher's primary intended audience: over age 15 / 9th grade to PN6700-PN6790; through age 15 / 9th grade to PZ. [sec. 1]
- Never class a comic book or graphic novel under an individual literary author in PB-PT, even an adaptation using the exact words of the original. [sec. 1]
- Prose fiction with occasional comic-character illustrations is a literary work (author number, or PZ if juvenile), not a comic. [sec. 2.b]
- Nonfiction in comic style goes with its topic, not PN6700-PN6790. [sec. 2.d]
- Photo novels go to PN6700-PN6790 even though they are photographs, while photograph collections without a narrative go to NC1300-NC1763. [secs. 2.a, 2.e]
- Prior to late 2007 all comic books and graphic novels were classed in PN6700-PN6790 regardless of audience; earlier records may reflect that. [Background]
