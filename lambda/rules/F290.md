## Applies when

- The schedule for the topic has a "General special" caption (sometimes qualified "Special
  aspects of the subject as a whole").
- The work's concepts cannot be precisely determined, or its subject is too new to locate in
  the hierarchy.

## Rules

1. Use of General special class numbers. Class the following types of material in General
   special numbers: [sec. 1]
   - "Amorphous" works for which it is impossible to determine precisely the actual concepts
     discussed.
   - Works involving subjects so new that it cannot immediately be determined precisely where
     they fit in a classification hierarchy.
   Do not class in General special numbers works whose contents can be precisely determined
   and designated. If the schedule does not provide for a topic in question, propose a number
   for it, even though the work in hand may represent the first work on the topic.
2. Special topics. Do not add Including notes to General special numbers to indicate what
   special topics are to be classed in the number. Establish specific numbers for special
   topics that can be identified, using a new class number. Do not convert a General special
   number to Special topics, A-Z. [sec. 2]
3. New developments. Do not establish new General special numbers. [sec. 3]

## Pitfalls

- A General special number is not a catch-all for topics missing from the schedule; a missing
  identifiable topic gets a new number instead [sec. 1-2].
- General special is distinct from General works (F 280).
