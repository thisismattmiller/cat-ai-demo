## Applies when
- The class number is in subclass PZ, chiefly PZ5-10.5 (juvenile belles lettres), and the work is by an individual author whose Cutter is subarranged by title work letters.
- A work in PZ is entered under title (no title work letters; Cutter instead).
- The work is a translation or a bilingual edition of a children's work (class with the language of translation).

## Rules
1. Title work letters are not Cutter numbers, because the capital letter is not followed by a numeral. Instead, the first letters of the title of the work (excluding initial articles) are used. They have been used primarily in the span PZ5-10.5; consult the LC shelflist to determine whether they have been used in other class numbers in PZ. [Background]
2. General rule. Consult the class number in the shelflist. Find the proper location for the work being shelflisted. Use the first two letters of the title (excluding articles) as the title work letters. Complete the call number by adding the publication date. Format: `PZ7.B1387 Lo 1988` (author Cutter, space, work letters with initial capital, space, date). [sec. 1]
   - When an author repeats the same initial words in the titles of multiple works, adjust the title work letters to achieve an alphabetical arrangement of titles. [sec. 1]
   - Title work letters may be expanded to three or more letters to achieve an alphabetical arrangement of titles. [sec. 1]
3. Second letter of first significant word is "a". Assign three work letters when the second letter of the first significant word in the title is a. [sec. 2]
4. Titles beginning with one-letter words. Assign the letter, followed by an a and add additional letters if necessary. [sec. 3]
5. Titles beginning with Arabic numerals. These file before titles beginning with letters. Begin the title work letter with Aa, followed by a third letter. [sec. 4]
6. Uniform titles. Base the work letters on the title proper, not on the uniform title. [sec. 5]
7. Works entered under title. Do not assign title work letters to works entered under title. Assign Cutter numbers instead. [sec. 6]
8. Translations. Class translations with the language of translation, not with the original work. A Spanish translation of an English-language children's novel classes in PZ73, not PZ7. Treat bilingual editions as translations: an English and Spanish bilingual edition of a work originally written in English classes in PZ73. [sec. 7]
9. Previous practices (found in the shelflist, not to be used for current cataloging): a single letter was used; numerals were filed as if spelled out; authors whose names began with Mc were filed as if they were Mac; and dates were not added. Use the rules in sections 1-7 for current cataloging. Do not revise records using previous practices. See G 100 for further information on previous shelflisting practices. [sec. 8]

## Examples
General rule: first two letters of the title, plus date [sec. 1]:
```
PZ7.B1387 Lo 1988    Baehr, Patricia Goehner. Louisa eclipsed
PZ7.B1387 Sc 1989    Baehr, Patricia Goehner. School isn't fair!
```

Same initial words in several titles: work letters adjusted to keep titles alphabetical (My dog and the key mystery was cataloged first; the others were adjusted around it) [sec. 1]:
```
PZ7.A2615 Mu 1987    Adler, David A. My dog and the birthday mystery
PZ7.A2615 Mw 1986    Adler, David A. My dog and the green sock mystery
PZ7.A2615 My 1982    Adler, David A. My dog and the key mystery
```

Expansion to three or more letters to keep titles alphabetical [sec. 1]:
```
PZ7.R79835 Haj 1999   Rowling, J. K. Harry Potter and the chamber of secrets
PZ7.R79835 Hal 2000   Rowling, J. K. Harry Potter and the goblet of fire
PZ7.R79835 Halm 2003  Rowling, J. K. Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix
PZ7.R79835 Ham 1999   Rowling, J. K. Harry Potter and the prisoner of Azkaban
```

Second letter of the first significant word is "a": three work letters [sec. 2]:
```
PZ7.L58418 Jaf 1983   Lewin, Hugh. Jafta
```

Title beginning with a one-letter word: the letter, then a, then more letters as needed [sec. 3]:
```
PZ7.B81263 Iaam 2002  Brown, Alan. I am a dog
PZ7.D29465 Paf 1997   Davis, Lee, 1941-  P.B. Bear's Christmas
```

Title beginning with an Arabic numeral: Aa plus a third letter, filing before letter titles [sec. 4]:
```
PZ7.C2146 Aah 2005    Carle, Eric. 10 little rubber ducks
```

Previous practices as found in the shelflist (single letter; numeral filed as spelled out; no date); do not use or revise [sec. 8]:
```
PZ7.S268 E            Sawyer, Edith A. Elsa's gift home
PZ7.B81667 Th         Brown, Paul. 3 rings
PZ7.R6435 Mi          Rolerson, Darrell A. Mr. Big Britches
```

## Pitfalls
- Work letters are letters only, never followed by a digit; do not turn them into a Cutter. [Background]
- Ignore initial articles when choosing the letters. [Background, sec. 1]
- The shelflist governs: the letters must be adjusted (or expanded) so that the author's titles stay in alphabetical order relative to what is already there; the letters are not simply the first two letters if that would misfile. [sec. 1]
- Second letter "a" in the first significant word forces three letters (Jaf, not Ja). [sec. 2]
- One-letter first words: letter + a + more letters (I am a dog -> Iaam; P.B. Bear -> Paf). [sec. 3]
- Numerals file first, under Aa + third letter (10 little rubber ducks -> Aah), not as if spelled out (old practice). [sec. 4, 8]
- Use the title proper, not the uniform title. [sec. 5]
- Works entered under title get Cutter numbers, not work letters. [sec. 6]
- Translations and bilingual editions class with the language of translation (PZ73 for Spanish), even when one of the languages is the original. [sec. 7]
- Do not revise older records that used single letters, spelled-out numerals, Mc-as-Mac filing, or no dates. [sec. 8]
