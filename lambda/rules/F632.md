  How to find or establish the class-P literary author number for works by or about an
  individual literary author (language, country, pseudonyms, name changes, authors writing
  in several languages), where non-literary works and criticism of one or several works
  are classed, and how author numbers are recorded in the 053 field.

## Applies when

- The 1XX/7XX names an individual literary author (a writer of fiction, drama, poetry, essays, etc.) and the work is by or about that author, so the class number is an individual author number or span in PA-PT (or PZ for juvenile belles lettres).
- The author has more than one name (pseudonym, joint pseudonym, changed heading), writes in more than one language, or is associated with more than one country.
- The work is a translation, a critical study, a biography, an index to the author, or a non-literary work by a literary author.
- The work is criticism of one work, of several works, or of a collection, and the author's table (e.g. Table P-PZ40) must be applied.

## Rules

Normally, all works by or about an individual literary author are classed together in the same number or span of numbers in class P regardless of the number of names under which the person may be entered and regardless of any changes in the form of heading for the person. Multiple numbers or spans may be established for authors who write in more than one language or for authors writing in English whose works are classed in PZ. The principal factors in locating an author number are the language in which the author wrote, the author's nationality, and, if required, the time period during which the author was productive. Authors living in the country most commonly associated with a particular language are classed with the general literature area for that language. Authors of another country may be classed by country in the area developed for that literature in other countries; many literatures have no geographic development, or only partial expansion, or special locations for collections but not for individual authors. United States literature in English is in subclass PS, not PR. (BACKGROUND)

1. Using established literary author numbers. [sec. 1]
   a. Searching. Search the name authority file, the LC database, the shelflist, and the P schedules to determine whether an individual literary author number has been established for the author. Search under the current heading, as well as under earlier catalog headings, pseudonyms, or other names. In name authority records, class numbers for literary authors are recorded in the 053 field. [sec. 1.a]
   b. Use of established name. If a number has already been established for the author, use it. For example, for Buck, Pearl S. (Pearl Sydenstricker), 1892-1973, use the full number PS3503.U198. [sec. 1.b]
   c. Name changes for authors with established numbers. If the name of an author with an existing literary author number changes significantly due to changes in cataloging rules, marital status, etc., continue to use the existing literary author number, if any, derived from the earlier form of the name. Submit a proposal to establish the number in the appropriate P schedule, together with a see reference from the new form of name (see sec. 3). "Changes significantly" means that the changes affect the filing order of the name. [sec. 1.c]
   d. Authors entered under more than one name. [sec. 1.d]
      (1) Single author using pseudonyms. Class works by or about an author who writes under several pseudonyms in the single number that has been established for that author, regardless of the name under which the work being cataloged has been entered. Submit a proposal to establish the number in the appropriate P schedule together with see references from the pseudonym(s) (see sec. 3). [sec. 1.d(1)]
      (2) Multiple authors using a joint pseudonym. Class works by or about authors using a joint pseudonym in the number appropriate to the joint pseudonym. Submit a proposal to establish the number in the appropriate P schedule. If one or more of the authors also publishes literary works independently of the other(s), provide cross-references (see sec. 3). [sec. 1.d(2)]

2. Establishing new literary author numbers. [sec. 2]
   a. Authors writing in one language. Class literary authors who write in only one language under the literature of the language in which the author writes or wrote, observing subarrangement by country or time period if provided. [sec. 2.a]
   b. Authors writing in more than one language. If an author writes in more than one language, establish a number under the literature of each language. Do not attempt to keep all the works of the author together. For a choice among the numbers established for such an author, see sec. 4.a. [sec. 2.b]
   c. Authors associated with more than one country. Once a decision has been made to class an author with a particular country, this number will usually continue to be used. Do not change the number unless the original choice was clearly in error. [sec. 2.c]
      (1) General rule. Class a literary author with the country of citizenship, if the literature of his or her language provides for arrangement of authors by country. If the author was a citizen of several countries, prefer the country in which the author's most productive years were spent, or the country usually associated with the author by scholars in the field. If no preference can be determined, make an arbitrary selection of a country. [sec. 2.c(1)]
      (2) Living authors about whom little information is known. Select a country based on available information (i.e., birthplace, parentage, residences, place of publication of the work(s) being cataloged). [sec. 2.c(2)]

3. Proposing literary author numbers for inclusion in the P schedules. [sec. 3]
   a. When to propose. Submit proposals to have individual numbers explicitly included in the P schedules for: [sec. 3.a]
      - literary authors whose name changes significantly due to changes in cataloging rules, marital status, etc. (cf. sec. 1.c);
      - literary authors born before 1925;
      - literary authors who have pseudonyms, regardless of date of birth. For pseudonymous authors, make a see reference from all names not used as the basis for the literary author number.
        Exception: Do not submit proposals for literary authors who use one name for works intended for children and another for works intended for adults, or for authors who use different names for different genres of juvenile works. Also do not submit proposals for literary authors whose pseudonyms correspond to the language of the work (e.g., an author uses the name Francisco Nieves when writing in Spanish and Francis White when writing in English).
      - literary authors who have joint pseudonyms, regardless of date of birth. If one or more of the authors also publishes literary works independently of the other(s), provide cross-references (see Examples);
      - anonymous works published before 1925.
   b. Formulating captions and see references. [sec. 3.b]
      (1) Form of name. For new authors, use the form of name established in the LC/PCC name authority file. For authors with existing numbers, use the form of name corresponding to the Cutter number or other alphabetical arrangement in the schedule. Do not list the various pseudonyms or alternate forms of names in parentheses or quotation marks after the name chosen as the caption.
      (2) See references. Make a see reference from any previously authorized form of the heading if it has a different entry element, from any pseudonym(s), or from the author's real name if the number is derived from a pseudonym. Do not make see references from variant names that are not pseudonyms but merely foreign language renderings of the name, even if the name authority record contains such references. Do not make see references from forms of the same name varying in fullness but all beginning with the same, or substantially the same, entry element.

4. Special cataloging situations. [sec. 4]
   a. Authors writing in more than one language, with more than one established number: [sec. 4.a]
      (1) Translations and critical studies. Class translations and critical studies of individual works with the original works.
      (2) Collective criticism of several works. Class according to the language of the group of works studied.
      (3) General criticism and biography. Class in the one predetermined number that best represents the total literary output of the author.
   b. Fiction works in English. Class works of fiction in English according to the general rules for literary works. Do not reclass earlier works classed in PZ unless the original cataloging is erroneous. Prior to 1980, LC classed works of fiction in English in PZ1-PZ4; this practice has been discontinued. [sec. 4.b]
   c. Non-literary works by literary authors. Class non-literary works by literary authors with the topic, unless a number for the work is specifically established in the literature schedules. Exception: If the work is a collection of essays on such diverse topics that it would normally class in AC schedule, class it in the author's literary author number instead. [sec. 4.c]

5. Criticism of the works of an individual literary author. Unless other instructions are provided in the schedules, class criticisms and commentaries of the works of an individual literary author as follows. [sec. 5]
   a. Criticism of an individual work. Classify a criticism of a work or collection classified as a Separate work with the text of the work or collection, following the guidelines presented in F 570 and G 340. [sec. 5.a]
   b. Criticism of multiple works or of a work classed as a collection. Classify a criticism of two or more literary works, or of a work classed as a collection, in the general criticism number(s) for the literary author (in Table P-PZ40: .Z5-999, Biography and criticism, General works). [sec. 5.b]
   c. Criticism of literary works for children. Classify criticism of literary works for children in classes PA-PM and PQ-PT according to the guidelines in sec. 5.a-b. Also classify in PA-PM and PQ-PT memoirs and biographies of the authors. [sec. 5.c]

6. Adding literary author numbers to name authority records. Literary author numbers are included in the 053 field of name authority records. For authors who write literary works under more than one name and who have authority records for each name, an 053 field is added to each record. [sec. 6]
   - For authors writing in the English language whose works are classified in PZ, add a qualifier to the classification number in the 053 field, using a $c subfield (see Tables). This does not apply to authors classed in PZ who write in languages other than English. Additional 053 fields may be added for authors of literary works for children if the author publishes in more than one PZ subclass. 053 fields may also be added if the author also writes for adults and has a literary author number in PA-PT, or if a literary author number in PA-PT has been assigned according to sec. 5.c.
   - When recording a span of numbers, give the full class number of the beginning of the span and the full class number of the end of the span ($a ... $b ...).
   - When an author writes in several languages and several numbers have been established, add a qualifier to each classification number, using a $c subfield. Do not enclose such qualifiers in brackets or parentheses.
   - 053 fields are not added for: authors whose literary number is expressed by successive Cutter numbers such as those generated by Table P-PZ16; authors whose works are classified in PN; authors established on undifferentiated name records.

## Examples

Established number used as is [sec. 1.b]:

```
PS3503.U198   Buck, Pearl S. (Pearl Sydenstricker), 1892-1973
```

Author writing in two languages gets a number under each literature; works are not kept together [sec. 2.b]:

```
PG3476.N3   Russian-language works of Vladimir Nabokov
PS3527.A15  English-language works of Vladimir Nabokov
```

Joint pseudonym with cross-references in the schedule [sec. 3.a]:

```
PS3601.L36   Alfonsi, Alice
             For works written with Marc Cerasini under the joint
             pseudonym Cleo Coyle see PS3603.O94.

PS3603.O94   Coyle, Cleo
             This is a joint pseudonym of Alice Alfonsi and Marc
             Cerasini. For works by Alice Alfonsi alone see PS3601.L36.
```

Criticism of an individual work (a novel classed as a Separate work) is classed with the text of the work [sec. 5.a]:

```
050 00 $a PS3562.A316 $b N36 2003
100 1# $a Lahiri, Jhumpa.
245 14 $a The namesake.

050 00 $a PS3562.A316 $b N3633 2010
100 1# $a Batra, Jagdish
245 10 $a Jhumpa Lahiri's The namesake : $b a critical study.
```

Note: The Namesake is a novel and is classed as a separate work.

Criticism of a collection known by its title (no conventional collective title, so a Separate work) is classed with the text [sec. 5.a]:

```
050 00 $a PS3525.A83 $b S5 2008
100 1# $a Masters, Edgar Lee, 1868-1950.
245 10 $a Spoon River anthology.

050 00 $a PS3525.A83 $b S535 1963
100 1# $a Hartley, Lois Teal, $b 1923-
245 10 $a Spoon River revisited.
```

Note: Spoon River Anthology is a collection of poems known by its title. It is not assigned a conventional collective title and is therefore classed as a separate work (see F 633).

A collection with a conventional collective title, Table P-PZ40, .A6 date [sec. 5.b]:

```
050 00 $a PS3562.A316 $b A6 1999
100 1# $a Lahiri, Jhumpa.
240 10 $a Short stories. $k Selections
245 10 $a Interpreter of maladies : $b stories.
```

[A collection of short stories with a conventional collective title. Table P-PZ40 applies. Use .A6 date, Selected works. Selections. By date]

Criticism of a work classed as a collection goes to general criticism, .Z5-999 [sec. 5.b]:

```
050 00 $a PS3562.A316 $b Z67 2002
245 00 $a Jumpa Lahiri, the master storyteller : $b a critical response
          to Interpreter of maladies.
```

[A criticism of Interpreter of Maladies, a collection of short stories with a conventional collective title. Table P-PZ40 applies. Use .Z5-999, Biography and criticism, General works]

Criticism of several works goes to general criticism, .Z5-999 [sec. 5.b]:

```
050 00 $a PS3562.A316 $b Z53 2010
245 00 $a Dynamics of culture and diaspora in Jhumpa Lahiri.
```

[A criticism of several of Lahiri's works. Table P-PZ40 applies. Use .Z5-999, Biography and criticism, General works]

053 fields in name authority records [sec. 6]:

Single literary author Cutter:

```
053 #0 $a PS3557.R48
100 1# $a Greth, Roma
```

Span of numbers for a single author (full class number at both ends of the span):

```
053 #0 $a PK1719 $b PK1727
100 0# $a Tagore, Rabindranath, $d 1861-1941
```

Multiple numbers for one author writing in several languages, each with a $c qualifier (not in brackets or parentheses):

```
053 #0 $a PK1947.9.T83 $c Awadhi
053 #0 $a PK1967.9.T8 $c Braj
100 0# $a Tulasīdāsa, $d 1532-1623
```

Author with successive Cutters (no single literary author number, no 053):

```
100 1# $a Frederick, Rawle   [053 field not provided]
```

Note: Rawle is a Bermudian author who writes in English, and is classed in the span PR9680.B43A-Z. Since the first cutter represents his country of citizenship, his name is represented by successive cutter numbers (i.e., the second cutter is different for each of his works), and he therefore does not have a single literary author number.

Children's author with a PS number for biography/criticism and qualified PZ numbers:

```
053 #0 $a PS3569.E6
053 #0 $a PZ7.S47 $c Juvenile fiction, English
053 #0 $a PZ8.3.S4684 $c Stories in rhyme, English
100 1# $a Sendak, Maurice
```

Note: Sendak is an author of literary works for children, and his works are classed in PZ. Biographies of Sendak and discussions of his work are classed in PS3569.E6. The 053 field provides his literary author number in PS. Additional 053 fields with qualifiers in subfield $c provide for his classification numbers in PZ--the first for his belles lettres and the second for his stories in rhyme.

Author using a different name for children's works: separate PZ Cutters per name, one shared PS number for biography and criticism:

```
053 #0 $a PS3558.A4636
053 #0 $a PZ7.H1925 $c Juvenile fiction, English
100 1# $a Handler, Daniel

053 #0 $a PS3558.A4636
053 #0 $a PZ7.S6795 $c Juvenile fiction, English
100 1# $a Snicket, Lemony
```

Note: Handler is primarily known by his pseudonym Lemony Snicket, and he writes literary works for children under both names. Works written for children by Handler are classed in PZ7.H1925, and works written for children by Snicket are classed in PZ7.S6795. Literary author numbers are not proposed for inclusion in the P schedule for authors who use different names for works intended for children. Biographies of Handler and Snicket and discussions of his work are classed in PS3558.A4636.

Humorist classed in PN, no literary author number:

```
100 1# $a Hodgman, John   [053 field not provided]
```

Note: Hodgman is an American comic and his works of humor are classed in PN. He does not have a literary author number and an 053 field is not added.

## Pitfalls

- Search under earlier headings and pseudonyms before creating a number; an author entered under a new name keeps the number derived from the earlier form [sec. 1.a, 1.c].
- A single author with several pseudonyms has ONE number in PA-PT regardless of the name on the work [sec. 1.d(1)]; but in PZ each name on the work gets its own Cutter (F 615 sec. 4.b; Handler/Snicket example).
- A joint pseudonym has its own number; do not class joint works under one of the real authors [sec. 1.d(2)].
- Authors writing in several languages are deliberately NOT kept together [sec. 2.b]; translations and criticism of an individual work follow the original work's language, collective criticism follows the language of the group studied, and general criticism/biography goes to the one number that best represents total output [sec. 4.a].
- Do not change an author's country assignment unless the original choice was clearly in error [sec. 2.c].
- English-language fiction is no longer classed in PZ1-PZ4 (pre-1980 practice); do not reclass earlier PZ works unless erroneous [sec. 4.b].
- Non-literary works by literary authors go with the topic, except a collection of essays so diverse it would fall in AC, which goes in the author number [sec. 4.c].
- Criticism of one work (or of a collection known by its title, i.e. a Separate work) classes with the text; criticism of two or more works, or of a collection with a conventional collective title, classes in general criticism (.Z5-999 in P-PZ40) [sec. 5.a-b].
- Criticism, memoirs, and biographies of children's authors go in PA-PM/PQ-PT, not PZ [sec. 5.c].
- Schedule instructions override sec. 5 ("Unless other instructions are provided in the schedules").
- The 053 $c qualifier applies only to English-language authors classed in PZ [sec. 6].
