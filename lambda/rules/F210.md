## Applies when

- The record is for a serial or other continuing resource (periodical, numbered monographic
  series, society serial, yearbook, annual, regularly reissued publication).
- The serial is linked to a previously cataloged serial (title change, continuation).
- Reclassification of a serial run is being considered.

## Rules

1. Caption. The most common caption used for class numbers for serials is "Periodicals.
   Societies. Serials". Many variants exist, e.g. "Periodicals", "Periodicals, etc.",
   "Periodicals and societies", "Periodicals, societies, congresses, serial publications"
   (the Q schedule uses the last). [sec. 1-2]
2. When to establish a new class number for periodicals. Establish a class number for
   periodicals only as part of a proposal for a major new development for a topic on which
   many periodicals are likely to be published. Use whole numbers or decimal numbers instead of
   "A" Cutters. Establish numbers for all forms of material likely to be received. Do not
   establish a new periodicals number if there are periodicals on the topic already classed in
   the number for general works (it is preferable to keep periodicals intermingled with general
   works than to perpetuate an incorrect number or reclassify serial runs). [sec. 3]
3. Use of the periodicals class number. Unless other specific provisions are printed in the
   schedules, class all serial publications, including periodicals, numbered monographic
   series, topical serial society publications, and other continuing resources in this number
   (see F 225 for further information on society publications). [sec. 4]
   a. Yearbooks. Except where specific numbers for yearbooks are provided in class K, class
      yearbooks in the periodicals number. Numbers for yearbooks in classes other than K are
      obsolete, and have been parenthesized (cf. F 230). [sec. 4.a]
   b. Serials that are not periodicals. Class in the periodicals number publications that are
      cataloged as serials because they are revised and reissued in full on a regular basis.
      [sec. 4.b]
4. No periodicals number. If there is no class number for periodicals, class periodicals with
   general works. [sec. 5]
5. Serial title changes and continuations. If the serial is linked to a previously cataloged
   serial and the numbering continues, assign the same call number that was assigned to the
   previously cataloged serial provided that the class number is acceptable. An acceptable
   class number is a number essentially correct when originally assigned, even if the class
   number is no longer printed in the classification schedules due to schedule revision (cf.
   F 185). Treat serials lacking numbering that are identified only by year as "numbering
   continues." [sec. 6]
6. Reclassification of serials. Because of the work involved in reclassing, remarking, and
   reshelving large runs of serials, be very conservative in recommending that a serial be
   reclassed. [sec. 7]
   a. Reclass for gross errors. Reclass a serial only when a gross error was originally made,
      such as using a number that does not correspond to the major discipline of the work
      (e.g., Biology vs. Religion). [sec. 7.a]
   b. Do not reclass for schedule revision or judgement. Do not reclass in situations where a
      number no longer exists because of schedule revision or where the assigned number is near
      the correct number. Do not reclass because another number is judged to be slightly better
      than the one originally assigned. [sec. 7.b]

## Pitfalls

- Congresses are not classed in the periodicals number unless the caption explicitly includes
  congresses, as in class Q (F 240 sec. 1).
- Publications about a society (not substantive topical material) follow F 225, which may put
  them in a separate societies number.
- A continuation with continuing numbering keeps an unprinted (obsolete) number; a
  year-identified unnumbered serial counts as "numbering continues" [sec. 6].
- New periodicals numbers are never "A" Cutters [sec. 3]; cf. F 220 for the legacy .A Cutter
  arrangement.
