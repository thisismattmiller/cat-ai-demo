## Applies when

- Every Cutter built from a heading or title: the filing position of the 1XX and/or 245 among existing entries determines the Cutter, so the heading must first be normalized by these rules.
- The heading contains diacritics or special letters, abbreviations, bracketed data, hyphens, an initial article, initials or acronyms, a prefix (De, Mac, Mc, D', El-), a suffix (Jr., III), numerals, fractions, decimals, dates distinguishing otherwise identical headings, an ampersand or other symbol, an apostrophe, or a compound surname.

## Rules

**Background.** When establishing Cutter numbers and filing new entries in its shelflist, the Library of Congress follows the principles of filing stated in the Library of Congress Filing Rules (LCFR) (Washington : Library of Congress, 1980). These rules have been "superimposed" upon previous filing arrangements: previously established or shelflisted entries have been retained as valid and only newly established or shelflisted entries have been filed according to the new rules. As a result, two filing systems can be observed within the classification schedules and the shelflist. The use of the old rules may be inferred when a call number lacks a date, because the routine use of dates in monographs did not begin until 1982. This chapter provides additional guidelines to be used in connection with LCFR.

1. **General principle** [sec. 1]. File elements in a filing entry in exactly the form and order in which they appear. Apply the "file-as-is" principle. Arrange the major components of a filing entry word by word, and then character by character. A word is one or more characters set off by spaces and/or marks of significant punctuation. Arrange individual letters according to the order of the English alphabet (A-Z).
   - Note: Before 1981 entries were usually filed as if they were spelled out in the language of the text.

2. **Modified letters** [sec. 2]. Treat modified letters like their equivalents in the English alphabet. Ignore diacritical marks and modifications of recognizable English letters. Special letters with no immediately recognizable equivalent file as shown in the Tables.
   - Note: Before 1981, letters with umlauts were filed as if followed by the letter e, e.g., ä was filed as if it were ae; ü was filed as if it were ue.

3. **Order of fields with identical leading elements** [sec. 3]. When the identical filing elements in a group represent different types of entries, arrange the group as follows: person, place, corporate body, title.

4. **Place names** [sec. 4]. When the leading elements of two or more place names at the beginning of a corporate name heading are identical, group the fields together as follows: place name alone, place name followed by a parenthetical qualifier, place name followed by a comma and additional words.

5. **Identical filing entries** [sec. 5]. Consider the title to extend only to the first significant mark of punctuation which will be either a period ( . ) or a slash ( / ). If two identical but unrelated title entries appear in the same class, arrange the entries by imprint date using successive Cutters, in order of receipt.
   - Note: See Library of Congress Filing Rules sec. 5.5 for information on the filing of titles with identical entry elements.

6. **Abbreviations** [sec. 6]. File abbreviations exactly as written. For corporate bodies whose headings were previously established with an abbreviation but are now established in spelled-out form (e.g., United States. Dept. of State is now established as United States. Department of State) see G 220 sec. 5.b.
   - Note: Before 1981 abbreviations were filed as spelled out in the language of the text.

7. **Bracketed data** [sec. 7]. If an entry includes bracketed data, treat it as significant for determining the filing position.
   - Note: Until the adoption of RDA in 2013, the word sic could appear within brackets. Bracketed data could also begin with the abbreviation i.e. In each case, the text in the brackets was ignored when determining the filing position of the entry.

8. **Hyphenated words** [sec. 8]. Treat words connected by a hyphen as separate words, regardless of language.
   - Note: Before 1981, hyphenated words beginning with prefixes such as "anti-" were filed as one word. Other special rules were also applied.

9. **Initial articles** [sec. 9]. Ignore initial articles in the nominative case that appear at the beginning of a main entry whether they appear separately or are elided. Disregard the definite and indefinite articles in filing in the shelflist. For a list of articles, see MARC 21 Format for Bibliographic Data, Appendix F, Initial Definite and Indefinite Articles. The languages listed in the Tables do not contain initial articles; it is not necessary to determine whether or not the first word in a title in those languages is an article.
   - Note: No attempt is made to differentiate cardinal numbers from articles when they are the same in any given language.

10. **Initial articles in place names and personal names** [sec. 10]. Treat initial articles that form a part of place names and personal names as an integral part of the heading and regard them in filing.

11. **Initials and acronyms** [sec. 11].
    - a. **With marks of punctuation** [sec. 11.a]. Treat initials separated by marks of punctuation and/or spaces as separate words.
    - b. **Without marks of punctuation** [sec. 11.b]. Treat acronyms and initials not separated in any way as single words regardless of capitalization. When initials and acronyms are followed only by ellipses or other marks of omission, disregard the ellipses or marks of omission. If an additional letter or letters follows the mark of omission, they are treated as if they constitute a new word.
    - Note: Before 1981 initials, whether containing punctuation or not, were filed before words beginning with the same initial letter. Hence FORTRAN filed before Fable.

12. **Names with a prefix** [sec. 12]. Treat a prefix that is part of a name or place as a separate word unless it is joined to the rest of the name directly or by an apostrophe without a space. File letter by letter.
    - Note: Before 1981 personal and place names with prefixes were filed as one word regardless of the actual spelling. This enabled names such as La Salle and Lasalle to file together.
    - Note: Before 1981 names with M', Mc, and Mac were all interfiled as if they were spelled Mac.

13. **Names with a suffix** [sec. 13]. Treat a suffix that is part of a name as a separate word. File suffixes containing numerals before letters of the alphabet, arranged according to their numerical value.

14. **Numerals** [sec. 14].
    - a. **Numbers expressed as words** [sec. 14.a]. File numbers expressed as words alphabetically, e.g. file seventy-six trombones under "S," not under "7."
    - b. **Numbers expressed in digits or other notation** [sec. 14.b]. File numbers expressed in digits (e.g. 1243) or other notation (e.g. XXVII) before letters of the alphabet. Arrange such numbers according to their numerical value. Assign a Cutter number in the A12-19 range for these entries. Normally, entries beginning with numerals should appear before entries beginning with the letter A.
      - Note: Before 1981, numerals in titles were arranged as if spelled out in the language of the rest of the title.
    - c. **Numerals after a decimal point** [sec. 14.c]. Arrange numerals after a decimal point digit by digit, one place at a time. Arrange decimal numerals that are not combined with a whole numeral (e.g., .45) before the numeral 1. Arrange decimal numerals that are combined with a whole numeral after all entries with the same whole numeral alone.
    - d. **Fractions** [sec. 14.d]. Since fractions are perceived in the online catalog as two numbers, file them behind any arabic numerals and after any Roman numerals as the numerical value would indicate (e.g., 1/4 files after the arabic numeral 1 and after the Roman numeral I) (cf. sec. 13.c, above — as printed).
    - e. Comprehensive example: see Examples.

15. **Chronological arrangements** [sec. 15]. When numbers or dates distinguish between otherwise identical entries, use a numerical or a chronological arrangement, rather than an alphabetical arrangement.

16. **Ampersand and other symbols** [sec. 16]. The ampersand (&) is the only symbol that has filing value. It follows spaces and precedes the lowest Arabic numeral or alphabetic character. Ignore all other symbols when filing into the shelflist.

17. **Words with apostrophes** [sec. 17]. Arrange elided words, possessives and other words with apostrophes as one word.
    - Note: When the first part of the elision is an article in the nominative case, it is generally ignored.

18. **Compound names** [sec. 18]. File single surnames before compound surnames.

## Pitfalls

- Two filing systems coexist in the shelflist; entries lacking a date (pre-1982) may have been filed under the old rules (abbreviations and numerals spelled out, Mc/Mac interfiled, prefixes run together, umlaut = ae/ue, initials before words). New entries follow the current rules but must still be fitted among the old ones [Background; Notes to secs. 1, 2, 6, 8, 11, 12, 14].
- A title is read only to the first period or slash for identity; identical unrelated titles are distinguished by imprint date with successive Cutters, in order of receipt [sec. 5].
- Initial articles are ignored only in the nominative case at the beginning of a main entry; in personal and place names (El-Ad, Las Lomas) they file [secs. 9, 10]. Elided articles (L', D') at the start of a title are generally ignored [sec. 17 Note].
- Bracketed data now counts for filing ("Duo [for] violin"); before RDA (2013) [sic] and [i.e. ...] were ignored [sec. 7].
- Hyphens split words, so "Anti-aircraft" files as two words before "Anticaglia" [sec. 8].
- "De La Cruz" (spaces) files before "DeAndrea" (joined) because spaces file before letters [sec. 12].
- Numerals: digits file before letters and by numerical value, decimals (.303) file before 1, fractions after the whole number, Roman numerals by value (IV between 3 and 838); numbers spelled as words file alphabetically; Cutter these numeral entries in .A12-.A19 [sec. 14].
- The ampersand is the only symbol with filing value; "&" sorts after a space and before "9" or "a" [sec. 16].
- Sec. 14.d (fractions) ends with the cross-reference "(cf. sec. 13.c, above)"; quoted as printed (the decimal rule it refers to is printed as sec. 14.c).
